Hog Charts

Glossary

Core Terms

Possession
One trip down the floor. A possession ends when a team shoots, turns the ball over, or goes to the free throw line. An offensive rebound doesn't start a new one. It extends the possession they're already in.
Per-100 Stats
Used as a pace-regularized alternative to per-game or per-36 minutes stats. In one game, both teams will play with the same pace, so pace-regularization is not necessary. However, if you want to compare stats between two random teams, per-100 stats are the best measure of their efficiency per possession, no matter how fast or slow they play.
Pace
Possessions per game. A higher pace means the team plays faster.
ORtg (Offensive Rating)
Points scored per 100 possessions.
DRtg (Defensive Rating)
Points allowed per 100 possessions.
Net Rating
ORtg - DRtg. The best measure of a team's efficiency: how many points better (or worse) they are against their opponents per 100 possesions.
FG% / 3P% / FT%
The percentage of two-point or three-point shots made (FG%), the percentage of three pointers made (3P%), and the percentage of free throws made (FT%).
eFG% (Effective FG%)
Field goal percentage adjusted for the fact that a three is worth more than a two. A made three counts as one and a half made twos.
TS% (True Shooting %)
Shooting efficiency counting twos, threes, and free throws together. It answers how many points a player produces on a typical scoring attempt.
PPS (Points Per Shot)
How many points a shot is worth on average, given where it was taken from. A 40% three-point shooter gets 1.20 points per shot; a 55% finisher at the rim gets 1.10.
Baseline / PPS±
Baseline is what an average Division I team shoots from that spot. PPS± is points per shot above or below that baseline, so it credits a shooter for being better than average where he actually shoots.
3PAr (3-Point Attempt Rate)
The share of shots that are three-pointers. A team can have a high rate and a poor percentage.
FTr (Free-Throw Rate)
Free throw attempts per field goal attempt, or how often they get to the line.

Player Stats

PPG / RPG / APG / SPG / BPG / TOV / MPG / GP
Points, rebounds, assists, steals, blocks, turnovers, and minutes per game, plus games played. Season totals divided by games played.
USG% (Usage)
The share of his team's possessions a player uses while he's on the floor: his shots, trips to the line, and turnovers. The five players on the floor add up to about 100%, so 20% is an average load and 30% or more is a primary option.
Resp% (Points Responsible)
Of the points his team scores while he's on the floor, the share he either scored himself or assisted. A scorer and his passer both get credit, so a team's numbers add up to more than 100%. Regular season only.
AST% (Assist %)
The share of his teammates' made field goals that a player assisted while he was on the floor.
TOV% (Turnover %)
Turnovers per 100 possessions the player used. Lower is better.
ORB% / DRB% / TRB%
Offensive, defensive, and total share of the available rebounds a player grabbed while he was on the floor. It accounts for how many rebounds were actually there to get, which rebounds per game does not.
STL% / BLK%
Steals per 100 opponent possessions, and the share of opponent two-point attempts a player blocked. Both are measured over the possessions he was on the floor for.
Ast'd% (Assisted %)
The share of a player's own made field goals that came off an assist. Low means he creates his own shot; high means he's finishing what someone else set up.
Rim% / Mid% / 3PA% (Shot Diet)
Where a player shoots from, as a share of his field goal attempts: at the rim, from mid-range, or from three. The three add up to 100%. Current season only.
On/Off
How much better or worse the team's net rating is with a player on the floor than with him off it. It also reflects who he plays alongside and against, so a big number doesn't prove he caused it.
RAPM / Off RAPM / Def RAPM
An estimate of what a player is worth in points per 100 possessions, separated from his teammates and opponents by looking at every combination of players who shared the floor. Off and Def split that into what he adds on each end. 0 is an average player.
Poss (RAPM sample)
How many possessions are behind a player's RAPM. More possessions means a more trustworthy number.
Percentile shading
Where a player ranks against every qualified Division I player, from 0 to 100, with 100 being the best. Players under 10 games or 10 minutes per game are too small a sample to rank, so theirs are hidden.
Scopes (All / Regular / Postseason / Conference / Non-Conference)
Which games are counted. All is the regular season plus the postseason. Regular and Postseason split those two. Conference is league games only, and Non-Conference is everything else in the regular season. Conference tournament games count as postseason.

Team Stats

Net / Off / Def
Offensive, defensive, and net rating, adjusted for the quality of the opponents faced. A team that puts up 110 per 100 against the best defenses in the country rates ahead of one that does it against the worst.
SOS (Strength of Schedule)
The average quality of the opponents a team has played, on the same scale as net rating. Higher means a tougher schedule.
Home
What a team's home floor is worth to them in points, based on how much better they play at home than on the road. Blank in the postseason, where games are on neutral floors.
PPP / Opp PPP
Points scored and points allowed per 100 possessions, straight from the box score. Unlike Net, Off, and Def, these are not adjusted for the opponent.
Four Factors (eFG% / TOV% / ORB% / FTr and D-versions)
The four rates a game usually comes down to: shooting (eFG%), turnovers (TOV%), offensive rebounding (ORB%), and getting to the line (FTr). Each has a defensive version for what opponents managed against you.
D eFG% / D 3P% / D TOV% / D FTr
The same stats, measured on what opponents did against this team. Lower is better on all of them except D TOV%, since forcing turnovers is a good thing.
A:TO (Assist-to-Turnover)
Assists divided by turnovers.

Scout

Gravity
A 0 to 100 score for how much attention a shooter demands beyond the arc. It combines how often he shoots threes with how well he makes them, and leans slightly toward volume.
Tiers (DON'T LEAVE / CLOSE OUT / RESPECT / SAG OFF / IGNORE)
How to guard a shooter, in plain language, from DON'T LEAVE down to IGNORE, based on his three-point volume and accuracy together. A * means he hasn't taken enough threes yet to be confident.
Shot Diet — Share / D-I Avg / vs Avg
Share is where this team's shots come from. D-I Avg is where an average team's shots come from. vs Avg is the gap between them.
Shot Quality — Att / FG% / Baseline / PPS / PPS±
How well they shoot from each spot. Att is attempts, Baseline is what an average team shoots there, and PPS± is how many more points per shot they get than average from that spot.
Scored% / Assist% / On-Court Pts
The two halves of Points Responsible: the share of his team's on-court points a player scored himself, and the share he assisted. On-Court Pts is the total his team scored while he was playing.
DRtg On / DRtg Off / On−Off
Points the team allows per 100 possessions with a player on the floor, and with him off it. A positive On−Off means the defense is worse with him out there.
Def Poss
Defensive possessions a player has been on the floor for. This is the sample size behind On−Off and Fouls/100. Small numbers move around a lot.
Fouls/100
Personal fouls per 100 defensive possessions. A high rate is a reason to attack him and put him in foul trouble.
Reb/100
Total rebounds per 100 possessions a player was on the floor for. This is raw volume, unlike the rebound percentages.
By Side (Left / Center / Right)
Attempts, accuracy, and points per shot for the left, center, and right of the floor. A wide gap is a reason to force him one way.
Recent Form (Season / Recent / Δ PPS)
Season-long shot mix and points per shot next to the same numbers from the last five games, so you can see whether a team has changed lately. Δ PPS is the difference between the two.

Lineups

Lineup
The players in this unit.
Poss
Possessions this lineup played together — the sample size. Bigger = more reliable.
Mins
Total minutes this lineup played together.
NetRtg
Net rating: points scored minus points allowed per 100 possessions with this lineup on the floor. Higher is better.
ORtg
Offensive rating: points scored per 100 possessions. Higher is better.
DRtg
Defensive rating: points allowed per 100 possessions. Lower is better.
AST / TOV / REB / STL / BLK
Assists, turnovers, rebounds, steals, and blocks per 100 possessions.

Shot Charts

Zones
The court is split into 14 areas: the restricted area under the rim, a ring of short shots around it, a mid-range ring, and five three-point zones running corner to corner.
Zone colors
Each zone is colored by how they shoot there compared with the Division I average for that same zone. A zone can be green at 38% from three and red at 55% at the rim. Green is above average, red is below, and the scale maxes out at 15 percentage points either way.
Minimum attempts
Zones with only a handful of attempts are hatched instead of filled in. There aren't enough shots there to read anything into.

Postseason

+/− (Point Differential)
Points scored minus points allowed across a team's tournament games.
